Past work · 2024–2025 · A project by Iftekhar Ahmed

Diagnos

Making personalized health guidance easier to access.

I co-founded Diagnos with Thun Silpsamrit at Rice. We wanted to bring nutrition, sleep, activity, and wearable data together so people could understand their health beyond a collection of separate numbers.

We started with something tangible: take a photo of a meal, understand its estimated nutrients, and decide what to eat next.

Starting with food

Logging food was tedious. Diagnos used meal photos alongside Rice’s dining menus to estimate nutrition, with dining-location context helping narrow down what was on the plate.

We built meal histories, nutrient breakdowns, and personalized suggestions around the foods available on campus. The aim was to make the next meal easier to choose, not just display yesterday’s calories.

In the demo, a scanned meal becomes an estimated nutrient breakdown. The app then brings together activity and recovery information, suggests meals, and shows experimental camera-based wellness features.

A larger health picture

Nutrition was our entry point. The broader vision was to connect intake with sleep, recovery, activity, and personal goals. We worked with Apple Health data and explored additional tracking integrations, with personalized insights as the link between them.

Some of that work reached the app; other features remained prototypes or plans. Our ambition was a useful daily health companion, not a replacement for clinical care.

Learning at Rice

We launched the nutrition app in September 2024 and reached over 1,000 users. Students tried it across the Rice community, including for food logging and feedback in a nutrition course. Having people return to something we had built in our dorm rooms made the work real.

The Rice Thresher covered the launch in October 2024.

From nutrition to athletic wellness

Conversations with Rice Athletics led us toward a different workflow: helping staff bring athletes’ nutrition, wearable measurements, recovery, and wellness information into one place.

We began developing an athlete-monitoring product and exploring AI-assisted analysis for staff. It was a substantial change—from an individual nutrition app to software for an athletic department—with different users, integrations, and decisions to support.

What I took from it

Diagnos taught me to protect the simple thing people already find useful. The larger health vision kept pulling us toward more features, while many people had come for a better nutrition tool.

I also learned that a pivot changes more than the product. A credible opportunity still needs a team that wants to pursue that particular problem. Those lessons now shape how I’m focusing Qube.
