TraceDog

AI-assisted investigations with evidence you can follow.

TraceDog follows claims through public records, web archives, and social sources, assembling findings with their supporting evidence.

Why it matters

Finding a page is easier than deciding whether it supports a claim. Records may contradict each other, refer to different people, or leave important gaps. TraceDog explores how an agent can make those distinctions clear enough for someone to review.

What I’m exploring

I’m interested in the work between finding a page and reaching a conclusion. Does a source support the claim? Do two records refer to the same person? What contradicts the story, and what is still unknown?

Evidence you can follow

The goal is a report that keeps sources attached to findings and makes uncertainty visible. Public records, articles, websites, and supplied documents become material to examine, rather than facts to accept automatically.

From a submitted claim to a report

The current workflow starts with a document or text submission. An investigator follows the claims into public sources and records findings with the evidence that supports them. A finding can remain partial, unresolved, or contradicted; a report should preserve that distinction.

I have been working on the handoff between research and publication. The system captures sources while the investigation runs, freezes the research state when it commits, and builds the report from that saved record. That makes it possible to check which sources belong to a finding and which version of the investigation produced the report.

This work sits alongside Lenze, where I explore how agents understand changing visual information.

It’s an evolving side project in agent-driven research and verification.
