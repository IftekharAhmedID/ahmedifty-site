Lenze AI

Visit Lenze.ai ↗

Real-time vision for AI agents.

Lenze turns continuous visual streams into context an agent can use: what is happening, what changed, and what came before.

Why it matters

An agent acting over time needs more than a single screenshot. It needs to notice events, track changes, and remember enough context to understand when to act.

What I’m exploring

Imagine an AI playing online poker: it needs to know when to act, observe how opponents play, and remember previous hands. A system watching a grocery store needs to follow people, movements, and interactions as they unfold.

Lenze explores how continuous visual streams can become structured, context-aware understanding. A single image captures a moment; an agent working over time also needs to track changes and retain the context of earlier events.

What an agent needs to remember

In the poker example, seeing the current cards is only part of the task. The agent also needs the order of earlier actions, the state of the table, and whether it is now its turn. In a store, it needs to follow the same person across several moments instead of treating every frame as an unrelated image.

Those examples guide my work on Lenze: deciding which visual changes matter and how to keep enough context for the next decision. They describe the behavior I am exploring, rather than a benchmark or a finished deployment.

Lenze connects my interest in computer vision with the agent research behind TraceDog. For my other work, see research and publications.
