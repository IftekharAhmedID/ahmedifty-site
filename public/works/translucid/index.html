Translucid.cloud

The protected-session infrastructure that made Qube possible.

Translucid began as infrastructure other platforms could embed: a secure, interactive cloud workspace for assessments, interviews, and sensitive remote work.

The browser, terminal, or development environment would run in an isolated cloud session. Keyboard and mouse input would travel to that environment; its screen output would return through an encrypted media stream and browser-supported protected playback.

Building the foundation

I worked on cloud isolation, session policies, DRM/CDM-protected streaming, input relay, and the lifecycle of disposable workspaces. The practical questions were as important as the architecture: startup time, latency, reconnecting, and differences between browsers and operating systems.

As a standalone product, Translucid was intended to let a developer keep their own application and add this protected session layer underneath it.

Why it became Qube

Different platforms needed different environments, integrations, and policies. The technology had many possible uses, but that made it difficult to focus the product and give someone a simple starting point.

I took the infrastructure into Qube: one meeting experience with a shared cloud computer. Qube was born from Translucid’s work, and now gives that foundation a focused application.
